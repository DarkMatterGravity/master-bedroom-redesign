# Bed-wall ocean painting

`ocean-expressionist.jpg` is the selected project asset, generated using the
built-in `image_gen` tool and saved as a high-quality JPEG for the viewer.
It is used only on the large bed-wall canvas, with the existing black frame
and gallery fixture. Use case: stylized-concept.

Prompt:

> Create a finished contemporary abstract expressionist painting as a flat, edge-to-edge canvas texture, wide horizontal format approximately 2.2:1. Dramatic, sophisticated, gestural evocation of the ocean and crashing waves, strongly ABSTRACT rather than a literal seascape. Broad sweeping arcs of deep midnight navy and charcoal blue, smoky slate, muted blue-green seafoam, broken ivory and warm cream impasto; restrained tiny warm sand/ochre accents. Energetic palette-knife strokes, layered dry brushing, scraped paint, thick tactile paint ridges, luminous foam-like strokes cutting through dark masses. A dynamic asymmetric composition, large confident marks, a strong dark-to-light movement, calm negative space to balance the drama. It should evoke the force and rhythm of water without depicting recognizable objects or a photographic wave. No boats, figures, buildings, beach, text, signature, border, frame, room mockup or perspective. The entire image is the artwork, evenly photographed straight-on for use as a wall-canvas texture in a realistic bedroom. Refined gallery quality, rich material texture, not a digital wallpaper pattern.


## Tall Sandy Hook nautical chart

`sandy-hook-1784.jpg` is a portrait detail of the historic **A chart of the bar
of Sandy Hook, the entrance of Hudson's River in the Province of New Jersey**,
surveyed in 1782 by John Hills and published by William Faden in 1784.
Public domain; Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division,
G3812.S3 1782 .H5, digital ID g3812s.ar129300.

- Catalog: https://www.loc.gov/item/74692777/
- Download: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_chart_of_the_bar_of_Sandy_Hook_the_entrance_of_Hudson%27s_River_in_the_Province_of_New_Jersey;_LOC_74692777.jpg

The chart is cropped around Sandy Hook and the surrounding soundings to fit
the existing 20-by-50-inch opening, preserving its geographic proportions.
The warm paper and engraving are from the original scan.
